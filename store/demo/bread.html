Why Bread Rises

3 min read

Yeast is a living organism. Given flour, water and a little warmth, it feeds on sugars and releases carbon dioxide. Gluten, a stretchy network formed when flour meets water, traps those bubbles, and the dough slowly grows.

Kneading builds that network, and resting gives the yeast time to work. In the oven, the gas expands one last time before the crust sets, which is why a good loaf keeps its shape and its open, airy crumb.
