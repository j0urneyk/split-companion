How Lighthouses Work

3 min read

A lighthouse gathers the light of a single lamp into a narrow, powerful beam. Rings of glass prisms bend the light so that almost none of it is wasted, and the beam can be seen many kilometres out at sea.

Each lighthouse flashes in its own pattern. Sailors read the rhythm, the colour and the pause between flashes to know exactly which light they are looking at and where the coast lies.
